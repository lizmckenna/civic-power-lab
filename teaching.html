Teaching philosophy

What we try to do in a classroom.

Teaching democracy at a policy school is a specific responsibility. Many students will soon be making decisions that shape whether other people have real voice, in campaigns, in agencies, in philanthropy, in movements.

Our courses take that seriously. We read rigorously, we fight about the readings, and we spend time on what civic work actually looks like on a Tuesday afternoon in a church basement.

The other non-negotiable: practitioners belong in the room. Every course has movement leaders, organizers, and former campaign staff as co-teachers and guests, not as decoration.

Courses
Academic year 2025, 2026
DPI-381Harvard Kennedy School

Organizing: People, Power, Change

Spring · MPP / MC-MPA · Capped at 60

A foundational course in community organizing, movement history, and the practice of collective leadership. Part lecture, part workshop, students leave having designed their own organizing project.

Sample unit topics

  • Narrative, public story, and the self in organizing
  • One-on-ones as a research method
  • Strategy and the strategic chart
  • Campaigns vs. programs: the designer's choice
  • Electoral organizing and its discontents
  • The ethics of mobilizing other people's time
DPI-409Harvard Kennedy School

Democracy & Backsliding

Fall · MPP / PhD · Seminar, 20

A graduate seminar on the comparative study of democratic erosion , but approached from below. How do movements, parties, and citizens resist or accelerate backsliding? What can be learned across Brazil, Hungary, India, the Philippines, and the United States?

Reading spine

  • Levitsky & Ziblatt · Haggard & Kaufman
  • Case studies: Brazil 2018 – 2022, Hungary 2010 – 2024
  • Perception and propaganda (new lab work)
  • Movement responses to backsliding
  • The "democracy industry" and its limits
SOC-2070Harvard GSAS · cross-listed

Mixed Methods in Political Sociology

Spring · PhD seminar · 12

A practicum in research design. Students bring a project to the seminar and leave with a pre-registered mixed-methods plan , combining ethnography, surveys, networks, and experiments in productive tension.

Course products

  • Pre-registered mixed-methods design
  • Memo to a hypothetical field partner
  • One short research artifact (brief, dashboard, or ethnographic vignette)
EXECHKS Executive Education

Leading Civic Organizations in a Strained Democracy

Summer residential · 5 days · ~45 leaders

An executive program for directors of civic, advocacy, and movement organizations. Designed in partnership with practitioners. Small cohort, demanding schedule, candid conversation.

Past cohorts include leaders from

  • National organizing federations
  • Community-based research groups
  • Movement-aligned philanthropies
  • Civic tech and data organizations
IOP-123Harvard IOP · student-led

Reading Group: Movement Memoirs

Year-round · non-credit · open

An informal reading group for students interested in organizing as a craft. We read one movement memoir a month, Ella Baker, Marshall Ganz, Barbara Ransby, Keeanga-Yamahtta Taylor, and many less-cited others, over long dinners.

How to join

  • Open to any Harvard student
  • Next cycle begins September
  • Email civicpower@hks for the reading list
