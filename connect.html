Why a written spec?

A small act of reciprocity.

We used to default to "let's hop on a call." It mostly didn't work, calls are easy to schedule, hard to follow up on, and the actual ideas tended to get lost in the shuffle.

A short written brief works better. You take 30 – 60 minutes to tell us what you'd like us to study, why it matters, and what would count as a useful answer. We take the same 30 – 60 minutes to read it carefully and come back with a real yes or no within two weeks.

No need to polish it. Bullets are fine. We just want to know what you're working on and what you're trying to figure out.

Research spec

What a good spec contains.

There's no template police. But strong specs usually cover:

  • A clear research question, stated as a question
  • Why now, the decision it will inform
  • What you already know and what you don't
  • Access you can offer (people, data, sites)
  • What a useful output would look like
  • Timeline and funding posture
Our response time: within two weeks, always. Either a genuine yes, a genuine no, or a "yes but not from us, here's who."
Researcher interest

How we hire.

We maintain a single rolling form instead of cycle-specific postings. When positions open, we start with people already in the queue.

  • RAs: most years, 2 – 4 positions
  • Postdocs: annually, 1 – 2 positions
  • Visitors: sabbatical and fellowship year
What we look for: curiosity about method, some fieldwork experience, fluency in at least one research tradition, and a genuine commitment to working with organizations as equals.
