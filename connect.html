Why we ask for a written brief

Why we ask for a written brief.

We used to default to "let's hop on a call." It mostly didn't work, because calls are easy to schedule and hard to follow up on. (As Fred Ross said, organizing is ninety percent follow-up.)

We've found that a short written brief works better. You take 30–60 minutes to tell us what you'd like us to study, why it matters, and what would count as a useful answer. We take the same time to read it carefully and come back within two weeks.

We just want to know what you're working on and what you're trying to figure out.

Research spec

What a good spec contains.

Strong specs usually cover:

  • A clear research question, stated as a question
  • Why now, the decision it will inform
  • What you already know and what you don't
  • Access you can offer (people, data, sites)
  • What a useful output would look like
  • Timeline and funding
Researcher interest

How we hire.

We maintain a single rolling form instead of cycle-specific postings. When positions open, we start with people already in the queue.

  • RAs: most years, 2–4 positions
  • Postdocs: annually, 1–2 positions
  • Visitors: sabbatical and fellowship year
