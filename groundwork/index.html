01 · The work

Groundwork: an organizing stack owned by the field.

The dominant civic-tech platforms are owned by private equity, expensive, oriented toward large institutional clients, and designed to retain users rather than develop their practice. When organizations cannot systematically capture or learn from their relational data, field-wide learning is impossible.

Groundwork is the secure, open-source alternative: structured records of recruitment channels, one‑on‑one conversations, commitment follow-through, leadership development trajectories, organizers' weekly reports and coaches' responses, team formation and dissolution, and event participation, all linked to individual contact records and organizational outcomes over time. Field-owned and democratically governed.

Anticipated beta will roll out to 5–10 POLIS organizations in the next 12–18 months (the staggered rollout is also the research design).

02 · Why now

The window is narrow, and it is open.

Two things are true at once. Civic organizations need durable infrastructure heading into a high-stakes electoral cycle, and they will only build relational capacity now if the tools are in their hands now. And: AI‑assisted engineering has, for the first time, made it feasible to ship production‑quality alternatives to incumbent civic tech at a fraction of prior cost and timeline. That window will not stay open. Whoever ships the field-owned tier in 2026 sets the defaults for the next decade.

We are not a vendor. We are a research lab at the Kennedy School, and the only reason we are building software is because nobody else with our footing in the field is positioned to do it without investor incentives bending the product back toward extraction. Our familiarity with the research and our deep partner relationships are precisely why we can build it in an opinionated way, optimized for what actually builds democratic practice. A short, well‑capitalized push gets the platform to the partners already waiting on it.

Field-tested measurement

Power Metrics: becoming a field standard.

Three dimensions of power-building: base-building (leadership and commitment), collective capacities (solidarity, strategy, structure), and impact and influence. Developed through three years of practice-based research with twelve organizing groups in partnership with the Democracy & Power Innovation Fund, building on the foundation of Prisms of the People and now extended through POLIS.

“In short, the real work of building democratic power is not being measured, only individual participation. This means strategic investments are tied to and cover only the tip of the iceberg in terms of what has to be done to attain more robust democratic participation. All the work behind the result is invisible, but it shouldn't be.” — Cushman & McKenna 2023, p. 4

POLIS · the data backbone

The first large-N dataset built to study organizing.

A multi-year study of 38 state-based organizing groups across 10 states. Recruitment channels, one‑on‑ones, leadership trajectories, team formation at daily resolution. Working data is currently under DUA with partners; a public, de-identified release is planned once the embargo lifts.
