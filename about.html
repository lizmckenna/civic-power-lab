About the lab

The R&D arm of democracy.

The Civic Power Lab is a mixed-methods research lab at the Harvard Kennedy School, housed at the Center for Public Leadership for Democratic Governance and Innovation. We study, and help build, the organizational infrastructure that turns civic participation into political power.

We coined the phrase "R&D arm of democracy" to name a role the field needs somebody to play. A lab can't fill that whole space alone, but somebody has to insist it exists. We insist, and we work shoulder-to-shoulder with the organizations doing the actual work.

Mission & commitments

What we're here to do.

Produce rigorous social-scientific research on democratic practice , especially the organizations, movements, and civic ecosystems that sustain it, in close partnership with the people doing the work.

Build open-source infrastructure, training programs, and measurement tools the field can actually use, shipped alongside the research, not after it.

Train the next generation of researchers who move fluently between methods, and between the seminar room and the church basement.

Research in partnership. Partners help set questions, review outputs, and keep custody of shared data. No extraction.

Mixed methods, seriously. Ethnography and network analysis belong in the same paper if that's what the question requires.

Public-facing by discipline. Every project produces something intelligible outside the academy. Not as afterthought.

Collective authorship. We credit the people whose time and thinking made the work possible, and try to practice the collectivity we study.

Team

A lab is the people who show up to do the work.

Sociologists, ethnographers, network analysts, forward-deployed engineers, and a rotating bench of postdocs, RAs, and visiting scholars. Most of us have organizing experience outside the academy, and all of us believe the distinction is overrated.

Research partnerships

Six principles of partnership.

01

Research questions come from the work.

Partners help scope what we study. If it's not useful to the organizations doing the work, we're not doing it.

02

Nothing private gets published private.

Partners review every public output with our findings in it before it goes anywhere, journal, blog post, or press.

03

Payable time, not extractive time.

We budget honoraria for partner staff time the same way we budget for our own. Research isn't free labor.

04

Data sovereignty.

Partners keep control of data they help us collect. Joint custody of datasets, written into the MOU upfront.

05

We write memos, not just papers.

Every major project produces something a busy organizer can actually read, short, plain, and useful.

06

Long relationships over one-off studies.

Most of our partnerships run for years. Good research takes that long. So does trust.

History

A short timeline.

2014

Groundbreakers published

The book on the Obama campaign's 2.2 million volunteers (Oxford UP), the lineage the lab was built to continue.

2015, 2022

Pre-lab

Years of fieldwork in São Paulo and across US organizing groups. The partnerships that would become the lab's bench get built.

2021

Prisms of the People published

University of Chicago Press, the book-length case for leadership density as the core of civic power.

2023

The lab opens

Housed at the Center for Public Leadership. First POLIS site visits. Early conversations that become the Groundwork platform.

2024

Scaling up

Civic Perception panel launches in eight countries. Donors & Democracy begins with Solidaire.

2025

POLIS + Mamdani

POLIS grows to 38 orgs across 10 states. Rapid-response fieldwork during the NYC mayoral campaign. Carnegie Fellowship awarded.

2026

Groundwork alpha

First production deployments of the open-source relational organizing platform. First full partner convening in Cambridge.

Funders & institutional support

The lab gratefully acknowledges support from the Center for Public Leadership for Democratic Governance and Innovation, the Carnegie Corporation of New York (Carnegie Fellowship, 2025–27), the Democracy & Power Innovation Fund, the Ford Foundation, the Open Society Foundations, and several individual donors who prefer not to be named. Philanthropic support funds specific research streams, never findings.

The lab maintains full editorial and methodological independence. No funder has approval rights over research design, analysis, or publications.
