Exploration · logo small-size study

Making the snowflake read small.

The current Classic mark holds up from 24px up. Below that, the outer nodes collapse into the spokes and the whole thing gets mushy. The fix is not to cram more detail in, it's to design a sibling form that reads as a burst instead of a network when there isn't room for both. Everything here keeps the four-quadrant, one-center logic of the snowflake model, and every option uses the same accent-at-center move that marks the organizer.

01 · The problem

What breaks at 16–20px.

The Mini tier renders one center + four satellites + four spokes. At favicon sizes the satellites become 1–2px, the spokes thin below one pixel, and everything competes for the same quarter-inch of screen. You lose the network reading entirely. You're left with a blob.

02 · Six burst candidates

Sibling marks for the small tier.

The idea: keep the Full snowflake for signs, decks, hero lockups. Use a purpose-built burst sibling below ~24px. Every option below holds the accent-at-center rule, keeps a four-fold radial structure, and is drawn with enough mass that favicon sizes read cleanly.

03 · Favicon test

How each reads in the wild.

Rendering each candidate at 16px inside a mock browser tab. This is the real acid test, favicon placement next to a favicon-first competitor (Google, Harvard, NYT).

04 · Recommendation

Ship a family, not a mark.

The snowflake model is the conceptual backbone of the lab's work. We should not abandon it for a trendy burst at small sizes, and we should not squeeze the full network into a favicon. Use three siblings.

Mark A · 40px +

Classic snowflake.

The full 1 + 4 + 12 network, current Full tier. For letterhead, hero lockups, signage, slide covers, print. The canonical form.

Mark B · 24–40px

Leadered.

Current Mid: 1 + 4 with short tick-marks at each leader. Nav bars, email signatures, social avatars. Reads as network in a smaller footprint.

Mark C · ≤ 24px

Burst (pick one below).

Favicons, app icons, mobile. The small-size solution, same four-fold structure and center-accent as the family, but drawn with enough mass to read at 16px.
