Logo options — network as identity.

Eight snowflake- and network-inspired marks for the Civic Power Lab. The snowflake model is a beautiful fit for what the lab studies — distributed nodes, peer-to-peer ties, power that radiates outward from organized relationships — but it's also the most common civic-tech metaphor, so the job is to do it with enough specificity that it's ours.

What makes a network mark work
01 · Symmetry

Radial > axial

6-fold radial symmetry reads as snowflake, hub, star, and mandala at once. Keep the node count odd or six-fold — four nodes reads as "plus sign / medical."

02 · Weight

One stroke to rule them all

A single, consistent stroke weight across nodes and edges is what separates "logo" from "diagram." Thickness roughly 1/10th of the circumscribed diameter.

03 · Anchor

One memorable detail

A filled center dot, a second ring, a missing arc, a doubled spoke. Without one specific detail, any network logo dissolves into every other network logo.

04 · Scale

Survives the 16px test

Test every candidate at favicon size first. If you can't tell it apart from a generic asterisk at 16×16, it won't work in the nav bar either. Simplify until it does.

Recommendation

My pick: Option 05 for primary, Option 08 for favicon.

Option 05 does the most work per pixel. It is a snowflake, it's clearly a network, and the single terracotta node says something specific about the lab's relationship to the field — not the hub, not outside it, one node among peers. The single asymmetry gives it the "anchor detail" generic network marks lack.

For the 16×16 favicon, 05's asymmetry gets muddy — so switch to Option 08 at favicon size. Same palette, same six-dot skeleton, zero lines to crowd the pixels. A two-mark system where the big version is complex and the tiny version is radically simple is how most strong identities actually work.
