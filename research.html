POLIS, Participation, Organizing & Local Influence.

A multi-year study of 38 state-based organizing groups across 10 states, building the first field-wide evidence base for what works in civic power-building, and, in partnership with the Organizing Lab and the Democracy & Power Innovation Fund, the infrastructure for the field to learn from itself.

The question

What organizational practices, at what intensity, translate civic participation into political voice? POLIS builds the micro-level data that existing research on representation and movements can't yet access, recruitment channels, one-on-ones, commitment follow-through, leadership development trajectories, team formation , linked to longitudinal organizational outcomes.

Core hypotheses

  • Organizations that systematically track relational engagement and commitment follow-through develop leadership density faster than those that do not.
  • Structured organizing workshops increase collective agency and relational network density.
  • Leadership density predicts policy and electoral outcomes, consistent with the emerging POLIS thresholds.

Design

Mixed methods throughout: ethnography, semi-structured interviews, and participatory action research paired with quantitative analysis of the data generated by Groundwork (the lab's open-source relational platform, see Practice → Groundwork). The staggered rollout across the network, alpha (1–2 orgs), beta (5–10), full network, creates analytic leverage we pair with matching on pre-treatment covariates and links to public administrative data.

Partners, co-creators

POLIS organizations are co-creators, not subjects: they help scope the research, stress-test the platform, and co-interpret what the data reveals about their practice. In return they receive production-quality infrastructure, cross-organizational benchmarks, and direct input into platform development.

Stream 02
Active · Multi-year

The architecture of agency.

How do organizations teach people to believe their actions matter , and then act together, durably, even when the odds are long? A decade-spanning comparative study of organizing in the US and Brazil.

The question

Political participation has been studied for decades as an individual attitude. We treat it instead as something that gets produced, organizationally, in the lesson plans, turf meetings, 1-on-1s, and feedback loops that transform isolated frustration into collective action.

What we're doing

Longitudinal ethnography across six large-scale campaigns, three in the US, three in Brazil, combined with field-experimental tests of specific practices (relational 1-on-1s, public reflection, authorship of small campaign decisions) on durable agency.

Civic perception under strain.

Citizens read their democracy through the mood of a neighbor, the tone of the local news, the feeling at the grocery store. We study that, the everyday cognitive work of sensing whether democracy is well.

What we're doing

Annual panel surveys in eight countries paired with embedded conjoint and vignette experiments, testing which cues most shape citizens' reading of their own democracy's health.

Stream 04
Active · Applied

Donors & democracy.

Large-scale philanthropy is democratic infrastructure, whether or not it means to be. We map how funder strategy shapes, and sometimes displaces, the practice of democracy.

What we're doing

A mixed-methods map: 80+ program-officer interviews, content analysis of funder strategy documents, and network analysis of grant flows across a decade.

Stream 05
Active · Time-sensitive

Mamdani research initiative.

Tracing the organizational infrastructure behind the 2025 NYC mayoral campaign, and what it reveals about the future shape of urban coalitional politics.

What we're doing

A rapid mixed-methods study: canvass-data analysis paired with interviews of campaign staff, coalition leaders, and block-level volunteers. A companion network analysis traces which organizations brought which voters into contact with the campaign.
